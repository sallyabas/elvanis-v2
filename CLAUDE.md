## What this is
A goal-driven AI execution audit platform for founder-led B2B SaaS / tech-enabled SMEs (20–200 employees). Client picks a business goal, submits evidence (native tool exports or fill-in templates — no live integrations in V1), and the system runs five parallel AI-drafted lenses, which a human reviewer must approve before a client ever sees them. Output is a financially-quantified top-3-priorities + 30/60/90 roadmap, with an AI Opportunity section gated by a readiness check.

**Relaunch decision:** this ships under the existing Elvanis brand/domain (`app.elvanis.com`), not a separate company — but on genuinely fresh infrastructure (new GitHub repo, new Supabase project, new Vercel project). The old Elvanis app/DB is untouched, kept as reference/rollback, never shared or reused. Deploy on Vercel's free URL during build/pilot; DNS cutover to `app.elvanis.com` is the last step, done only after `RESEND_FROM_EMAIL` is verified working on the new project.

Market sequencing: UK → Netherlands/Europe → Gulf (gated on UK/NL proof).

## Non-negotiable architecture principles
- **No hardcoded provider calls.** Every LLM call goes through the internal `ai-client` abstraction (see `lib/ai-client/`) so switching model/provider is a config change, not a multi-file rewrite. Elvanis was already forced through one painful Groq migration — don't repeat that.
- **Mandatory human review gate.** A `reports` row can never reach `sent` status without passing `pending_review` → reviewer `approved`. This is enforced at the DB/workflow level, not just a UI convention. Every finding is `draft` → `reviewer_edited` (optional) → `approved` before it can appear client-facing.
- **Isolated infrastructure.** New Supabase project, new env vars, new domain path. Never touch the old Elvanis DB/repo/tables.
- **Evidence-in, structured-out, per lens.** Each of the five lenses is an independent AI call with its own strict output schema — one lens failing must not block the others.
- **Business profile is a living record.** Every lens prompt reads the *current* profile/goal/evidence state at generation time, never a cached copy from signup. Changes are tracked in `company_profile_history`.
- **Source-agnostic evidence intake.** No OAuth/live integrations in V1. Per-lens native export upload (CSV/PDF) or fill-in template, reconciled via a merge-and-complete step. A known-source template library (Xero, QuickBooks, HubSpot, Salesforce, Jira, Intercom, Zendesk) auto-maps recognized files; unrecognized files fall back to generic parsing.

## Generic module review architecture (confirmed 2026-08-02)
AI Reliability Audit, Tender Readiness, and Data Protection Compliance all hit the same question: how do their findings get reviewed? Solved generically once here, not as a one-off for AI Reliability with the other two repeating the question right behind it — same principle already used for AI & Governance not being forced into the shared `LensModule` interface: **reuse the review mechanism, don't force the findings shape.**

**Schema** (`supabase/migrations/20260802140000_generic_module_review.sql`) — replaces the three separate `*_requests` tables (`tender_readiness_requests`, `ai_reliability_requests`, `data_protection_requests`), all three still completely empty and unreferenced by any code (a v0 scaffold predating the lens architecture's findings-structure lessons), so dropping and replacing needed no backfill:
- **`module_requests`** — plays the same role `reports` plays for the core audit: the review-gate container, reusing the exact same `report_status` enum (draft/pending_review/approved/sent) and the exact same "sent requires a reviewer" DB check constraint. `intake_data` (jsonb) holds whatever raw intake shape that module needs — genuinely module-specific, doesn't generalize (AI Reliability's is `{systemType, conversationalTranscripts, agentAutomationEvidence}`; Tender Readiness/Data Protection will have their own shapes).
- **`module_findings`** — plays the same role `lens_findings` plays, reusing the exact same `reviewer_status` enum (draft/edited/approved/rejected) and the same immutable-`ai_draft`-vs-`reviewer_edited_content` pattern. ## Multi-jurisdiction regulatory landscape
Full research and sourcing in spec §1.8c — read that before building any jurisdiction-aware logic in Tender Readiness, AI & Governance, or Data Protection Compliance. Tender Readiness's own confirmed applicability design (which sections, the deterministic-in-code rule, the domain boundary vs. Data Protection Compliance) is spec §1.8b. Summary: regulatory applicability is two independent signals, not one "country" field. EU AI Act / GDPR / Saudi PDPL are extraterritorial (triggered by where *customers* are); UAE's DIFC Regulation 10 / ADGM rules are triggered by where the *company is registered* — both can apply to the same company at once, hence the `companies` schema split above. ## `goalRelevance` hallucination — root-caused and fixed, 2026-08-05

The `directly_supports` fix (2026-08-01) closed one real gap but was never the full story — `generateValidatedJson`'s safety net kept catching the same class of failure (Execution/Product/Commercial on 2026-08-04, see immediately above), which meant the underlying cause was still live, just being caught cleanly instead of corrupting data. Investigated properly this time rather than accepting "the safety net catches it" as good enough, per explicit direction not to leave this as a known gap.

**Root cause, confirmed by reading all three failing lenses' own prompts, not guessed**: every one of Execution's, Product's, and Commercial's own `GOAL-RELEVANCE GUIDANCE` sections describes the `cash_flow_margin_efficiency` goal in identical terms — a real, material, often-quantifiable COST or DRAG on the goal ("labor-hours cost of decision latency," "whether low adoption... is wasting acquisition spend," "pricing pressure forcing discounting") that is nonetheless not the finding's *primary* obstruction of the goal. Neither existing negative value honestly fits: `directly_blocks` overstates it (nothing is actually preventing the goal), `indirectly_affects` understates it (the cost link is direct and traceable, not tangential). This is the same class of gap `directly_supports` fixed, just on the negative side rather than the positive one — a schema incompleteness, not a prompt-wording problem, exactly as the original fix's own docblock already correctly diagnosed for its own case.

**Reproduced live, not just reasoned about**: built evidence at exactly this semantic boundary (meeting-load cost under Execution, low-adoption-wasting-spend under Product, pricing-pressure-forcing-discounts under Commercial, all under `cash_flow_margin_efficiency`) and ran each several times. Before the fix, identical evidence made the model swing unpredictably between `indirectly_affects` and `directly_blocks` across repeated runs — both wrong, and the instability itself (not a clean, consistent pick) is the same signature that originally motivated `directly_supports`. ## The one thing that cannot be verified internally: no real external pilot user yet (flagged 2026-08-05)

Every "verified live" claim in this file so far — the full client chain, all three modules, reviewer auth, real email, the reviewer-denial branch — was driven by the same person building the system, using real infrastructure but disposable or founder-controlled test accounts. That is genuine, real verification of *whether the system works as built*. It is not, and cannot be, verification of *whether the system works for someone who didn't build it*: unclear copy, a confusing intake question, an edge case in how a real company's messy real data doesn't fit the fill-in-template fields, a genuinely ambiguous goal-selection moment — none of these surface when the tester already knows what answer the system expects.

This is explicitly not something to "build" — there is no code fix for it. It is a gap in *proof*, not in the product, and it is the one V1 completeness claim that stays open no matter how much further internal testing happens. **V1 should not be considered fully proven until at least one real external user — someone other than the founder, with a real company and real evidence, with no foreknowledge of how the system is supposed to behave — has gone through the full flow (sign up → business profile → evidence submission → reviewer review → delivered report) and their experience has been observed, not assumed.** Flagging this now, explicitly, rather than letting continued internal verification quietly stand in for it.

## Working style
- Think like a CTO: scalability, dependencies, business impact — not just "does it run."
- Don't over-build ahead of proof. Exception: modules built from external research (Tender Readiness, AI Reliability, Data Protection) don't need a live client first — they're sequenced by engine-readiness, not by demand signal.
- Keep this file current. When real decisions get made or change in future sessions, update this file — don't let it go stale relative to the spec doc or actual code.
- **Never unilaterally drop, simplify, skip, or reduce the scope of a feature (confirmed 2026-08-03).** If something turns out harder than expected, ambiguous, or seemingly lower priority than other work, surface it clearly and ask — don't silently decide not to build it, and don't ship a reduced version without saying so. Reason: this session's own pattern showed real scope (the Saudi PDPL branch, the AI Reliability Audit design details, the regulatory-content-review gap) silently not happening simply because nobody re-raised it after the original document was written — the fix is flagging early, not assuming a checklist line will get picked back up on its own. This is a persistent rule, not a one-off — don't wait to be told again in a future session.
